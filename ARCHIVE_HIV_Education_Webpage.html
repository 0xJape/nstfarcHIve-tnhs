01. HIV basics

HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) is a virus that attacks the body's immune system. Without treatment it can gradually weaken the body's ability to fight infections. HIV is not the same as AIDS: AIDS is the most advanced stage of untreated HIV infection. With effective treatment, people living with HIV can live long, healthy lives.

HIV can spread through certain body fluids

  • Blood and some sexual fluids, when they enter the body through routes that allow transmission.
  • Sharing needles or other injecting equipment.
  • From parent to baby during pregnancy, delivery, or breastfeeding without appropriate prevention and care.

HIV does NOT spread through everyday contact

  • Hugging or holding hands.
  • Sharing meals, dishes, or drinking glasses.
  • Using the same toilet or common spaces.
  • Mosquito bites or casual social contact.

Remember: People living with HIV deserve the same everyday warmth, friendship, and respect as everyone else.

02. Testing

An HIV test is the only reliable way to know your HIV status. Many people have no symptoms. Testing can be done through health facilities and approved services, with counseling and follow-up care when needed. In the Philippines, HIV-related testing and information are protected by confidentiality provisions under RA 11166.

What should you expect?

  • Ask a qualified provider which HIV test is suitable for you.
  • A reactive screening result may require confirmatory testing.
  • Early diagnosis and timely treatment can protect long-term health.

Understand the window period

No HIV test can detect infection immediately after exposure. How soon HIV can be detected depends on the type of test. If you test too early and receive a negative result, you may need to test again after the relevant window period.

Important: If there may have been a recent exposure, ask for urgent medical advice about PEP rather than waiting for a routine testing date.

03. Prevention

HIV prevention works best when people can choose options that fit their lives, with information free from shame or judgment. Multiple evidence-based approaches can reduce transmission.

Prevention options

  • Use condoms correctly during sex.
  • Get tested regularly as appropriate and discuss STI screening.
  • Ask a healthcare provider about PrEP if you may be exposed to HIV.
  • Use sterile injecting equipment; do not share needles or syringes.

Treatment is also prevention

When a person living with HIV takes effective treatment and maintains an undetectable viral load, HIV is not sexually transmitted to partners (U=U). Condoms can still help protect against other sexually transmitted infections.

Remember: Prevention is about informed choices and access to care—not judging someone's identity or relationships.

04. PrEP and PEP

PrEP and PEP are different HIV-prevention medicines. Both involve advice from healthcare professionals, but the timing is very different.

PrEP — Before possible exposure

  • Pre-exposure prophylaxis is for people without HIV who may be exposed to HIV.
  • Depending on what is locally available and clinically appropriate, PrEP may be offered as pills or injections.
  • PrEP works when taken as prescribed; it does not prevent other STIs.

PEP — After a possible exposure

  • Post-exposure prophylaxis is emergency prevention medicine.
  • It should start as soon as possible, no later than 72 hours after potential exposure.
  • When prescribed, the usual course lasts 28 days.

Time matters: If you think you were exposed to HIV, seek urgent medical care now to determine whether PEP is appropriate. Do not wait for symptoms.

05. Treatment and U=U

Antiretroviral therapy (ART) helps control HIV by reducing the amount of virus in the body. With regular treatment and medical follow-up, many people living with HIV can stay healthy and enjoy long, fulfilling lives.

What does U=U mean?

Undetectable = Untransmittable. A person living with HIV who takes treatment and achieves and maintains an undetectable viral load does not transmit HIV through sex. Viral load testing helps confirm and monitor suppression.

Why follow-up matters

  • Take prescribed ART consistently.
  • Attend follow-up care and viral load monitoring.
  • Discuss side effects and treatment concerns with a healthcare professional.

Important distinction: U=U refers to sexual transmission. It does not mean HIV has been cured or that other routes of transmission require no precautions.

06. Respect and support

An HIV diagnosis does not define a person's identity, relationships, abilities, or worth. Stigma can discourage people from seeking testing and care. Respectful language, privacy, informed consent, and compassionate services matter.

What respect looks like

  • Say “person living with HIV,” not a label that reduces someone to their diagnosis.
  • Never disclose another person's HIV status without appropriate consent or legal authority.
  • Make room for questions without blame, assumptions, or fear.

Know your rights in the Philippines

Republic Act No. 11166, the Philippine HIV and AIDS Policy Act, provides important protections regarding confidentiality, voluntary testing, and discrimination. For advice about a specific situation, seek guidance from a qualified professional or appropriate organization.

A reminder: You deserve accurate information, support, privacy, and healthcare delivered with dignity.
